MacBook Won't Turn On? 5 Things to Try Before Booking a Repair

A surprising number of "dead" MacBooks aren't actually dead.

Dr. Fixit Mobile · Strathmore, AB · Updated September 2026

A black screen and an unresponsive MacBook feels like the worst-case scenario, but before you assume it's a logic board failure, there are a few checks worth trying — they take less than ten minutes combined and solve more cases than you'd expect.

1. Rule out a dead battery with a direct power test

Plug the MacBook directly into the wall with the original (or a genuine-spec) charger, wait 10-15 minutes, then try to power on. If the battery is fully drained, some models won't show any sign of life — not even the charging light — for the first several minutes of charging.

2. Check the charging cable and port, not just the battery

A worn-out charging cable or a port full of lint is one of the most common reasons a MacBook seems dead when the battery itself is fine. Look at the connector for bent pins or frayed cable near the plug, and inspect the port for dust or debris.

3. Try a different outlet or power source

It sounds almost too simple, but a dead outlet or a power bar that's switched off catches more people than you'd think. Try plugging into a different wall outlet directly.

4. Force a hardware reset

For MacBooks with Apple silicon (M1/M2/M3), hold the power button for 10 seconds, then release and press it again normally. For older Intel MacBooks, resetting the SMC (System Management Controller) can resolve power issues that look like a dead machine — the exact key combination varies by model and whether the battery is removable, so it's worth looking up the steps for your specific model rather than guessing.

5. Listen and watch closely for any sign of life

In a dark room, look closely at the screen for even a faint backlight glow, and listen for fan noise or a startup chime. A MacBook that's powering on but not displaying anything points to a screen or graphics issue rather than a dead board — a very different (and often cheaper) repair.

If none of this works: that's a good sign it's time for a proper diagnostic rather than more guessing — especially if the MacBook was recently dropped, exposed to liquid, or has a history of battery swelling.
